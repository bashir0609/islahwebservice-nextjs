## Account-Based Research: Building the Data Layer for ABM

Account-based marketing and sales are built on a promise that most teams never test: that you know your target accounts well enough to personalize against them. In practice, "knowing an account" usually means a CRM row with a logo and a stale contact list. The gap between ABM strategy and ABM execution is research.

This guide covers the data layer of ABM: how to research a named-account list, map the buying committee, enrich and verify records, and keep the data trustworthy as programs scale.

## Research the Account Before the Contacts

Account research is the step most teams skip. Before identifying people, a researcher builds a picture of each account:

- **Company profile** — what the company does, its size, geography, and ownership structure
- **Segmentation** — which tier, industry, and ICP band the account belongs to
- **Recent events** — funding, leadership changes, expansion, hiring, product launches
- **Signals** — the growth or technology indicators your team cares about
- **Structure** — subsidiaries, brands, and locations, so contacts map to the right entity

An account researched this way makes every downstream step cheaper: you know which entity to map, which signals to reference, and which roles are worth finding.

## Mapping the Buying Committee

ABM personalization needs more than one contact per account. The research should identify the roles that typically engage across the buying process:

- **Economic buyer** — the role accountable for the budget (often a C-level or VP)
- **Champion** — the person who wants the change and will advocate for it
- **User and technical evaluator** — the people who will use the product or vet it
- **Gatekeeper / procurement** — the roles that control access or process

A researcher maps the *current role holders* for each of these functions at the account. Mapping roles — not guessing personalities — is what keeps the research honest and repeatable across hundreds of accounts.

## Enrichment for ABM

For each mapped contact, the ABM data layer needs:

- Business email and LinkedIn URL
- Current title and the date it was verified
- Company, location, and firmographic data
- Engagement-ready fields: role function, seniority, and buying-committee position
- Custom fields your programs depend on

Enrichment fills the fields; verification protects the investment. Emails are checked for deliverability, roles are confirmed current, and records are deduplicated and standardized before delivery.

## The Limits of Account Research

Account research describes the account and the people in it. It does not:

- Confirm the account is evaluating a solution like yours
- Confirm any individual has budget authority
- Predict who will respond to outreach
- Guarantee the buying committee is complete

What it does is give your team a verified map of every named account — who is there, what their role is, and what signals the account shows. That is the data layer ABM needs; the outreach and personalization remain the team's work.

## Keeping the Data Trustworthy at Scale

ABM data decays quickly — roles change, companies restructure, and accounts merge. A trustworthy ABM data layer records a verification date on every field, supports re-verification cycles, and flags when a record has gone stale. Buying a list once and using it for a year is not ABM research; it's a data liability.

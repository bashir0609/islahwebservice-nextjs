## CRM-Ready Prospect Data: Cleaning, Deduplication, and Formatting

"CRM-ready" is the most abused phrase in B2B data. In practice it usually means someone pasted a spreadsheet into an import screen and hoped. The result is the reason CRMs rot: duplicates, inconsistent formats, missing fields, and records nobody can trust.

This guide covers what CRM-ready actually requires — the cleaning, deduplication, standardization, and verification work that turns raw prospect data into records your sales team can act on.

## Why Raw Data Fails in a CRM

Imported prospect data fails in predictable ways:

- **Duplicates** — the same company appears three times with different spellings, and the same person twice with different emails
- **Inconsistent formats** — phone numbers, job titles, and company names entered differently across sources
- **Missing fields** — records missing the fields your workflow depends on
- **Stale data** — roles and emails that were true when collected but not when imported
- **Wrong entity** — contacts mapped to the wrong company, brand, or subsidiary

Each failure has a cost: duplicates split your history, bad formatting breaks automation, and stale records poison your metrics. The fix is process, not hope.

## Deduplication Done Right

Deduplication is not "remove rows with the same email." It works at multiple levels:

- **Person-level dedup** — same person, multiple email formats or name variants
- **Company-level dedup** — same company, different legal names, trading names, or spellings
- **Account matching** — merging contacts onto the correct parent account
- **Cross-source dedup** — the same record arriving from different providers

The goal is one canonical record per person and per company, with the best available data merged in and conflicts resolved by rules you define.

## Standardization: The Invisible Work

Standardization is what makes the data usable:

- **Company names** — consistent legal vs. trading name handling
- **Titles** — normalized role functions and seniority levels
- **Emails** — verified formats and deliverability checks
- **Phone numbers** — consistent country codes and formats
- **Locations** — normalized city, region, and country fields
- **Custom fields** — every requested column populated consistently

Standardization is unglamorous and essential. It's also what makes reporting, automation, and enrichment work downstream.

## Field Mapping and Delivery

Before import, the data should be mapped to your CRM schema:

- Every source column mapped to the correct CRM field
- Lookup fields (company, owner, account) resolved to existing records
- Enrichment fields placed where your workflows expect them
- Validation rules run before import, not after

The deliverable formats matter too: CSV, Excel, and Google Sheets are standard, with custom columns exactly as your team requested. The point is that importing is an event, not a project.

## Verification: The Part That Protects Everything

Cleaning fixes what you have; verification confirms it's true:

- **Email verification** — deliverability checked at delivery time
- **Role checks** — the person still holds the title on the record
- **Company checks** — the account is active and correctly matched
- **Missing-field review** — gaps identified before delivery, not after

Every verified record should carry a verification date, so your team knows what was true when the data left the researcher's hands.

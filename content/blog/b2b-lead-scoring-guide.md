## B2B Lead Scoring

Every sales team has the same problem: too many leads and not enough hours. Lead scoring is the discipline that solves it — a transparent model that ranks prospects by how likely they are to buy, so your reps work the highest-value accounts first and stop wasting time on dead ends.

The good news is you don't need a complex AI system to start. You need a clear model, good data, and the discipline to review it. Here's how to build one.

## Why Lead Scoring Matters

Without scoring, pipeline is a lottery. With it:

- **Reps work the right accounts first** — highest fit and strongest signals
- **Marketing measures real quality** — not vanity volume
- **Handoffs get easier** — every account arrives with a rank and a reason
- **Win rates go up** — time is spent where the probability is

A good score answers two questions: *does this account fit?* and *are they showing interest?*

## The Two Sides of a Lead Score

### 1. Fit Score (Who They Are)

Fit is how well the account matches your ICP. Score each firmographic dimension:

- **Industry** — 0–3 points if they're in your target vertical
- **Company size** — 0–3 points if they're in your ideal employee band
- **Location** — 0–2 points if they're in your delivery zone
- **Tech stack** — 0–2 points if they use tools that complement yours
- **Budget signals** — 0–2 points for revenue or funding signals

Max fit score in this example: 12.

### 2. Signal Score (What They're Doing)

Signals show intent and momentum. Score observable behaviors:

- **Hiring the team you sell to** — 5 points (e.g., an [MSP prospect](/industries/msp) posting an IT support role)
- **New funding or expansion** — 4 points
- **New leadership in the buying role** — 3 points
- **Engaged with your content or site** — 2–4 points
- **Attending relevant events** — 2 points

A company can have perfect fit and zero signals — they're still worth tracking, just not first. Signals are what move accounts up the queue.

## Building the Model in Practice

**Step 1 — Define the dimensions.** Use the example above as a starting point and adjust weights to your deal.

**Step 2 — Set thresholds.** For example: 9+ total = priority, 6–8 = nurture, under 6 = long-term watchlist.

**Step 3 — Add negative scores.** Wrong industry, competitor accounts, or unresponsive-after-4-touches should *subtract* points.

**Step 4 — Review monthly.** Talk to reps about which scored accounts actually closed. Adjust weights on evidence, not instinct.

## Good Scoring Requires Good Data

A scoring model is only as good as the data feeding it. If firmographics are wrong or outdated, fit scores lie. If the emails are invalid, engagement signals are noise. ## What About AI and Predictive Scoring?

Predictive scoring tools analyze historical win/loss data to surface patterns humans miss. They're powerful — once you have the history to train them. For most teams, the pragmatic path is: build a transparent manual model first, gather six months of data, then let AI refine the weights. The manual model also forces a shared definition of "good lead" across sales and marketing, which is valuable on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's a good lead score?**
There's no universal number — it depends on your model and weights. Define thresholds that match your funnel and validate them against closed deals.

**Should sales and marketing score the same way?**
They should use one shared model, ideally built together, so both teams agree on what a qualified lead looks like.

**How often should I update scores?**
Re-score accounts whenever new signals appear (hiring, funding, engagement) and review the model itself monthly.

**Can I score without a CRM?**
Yes — a spreadsheet works for small volumes. But scoring compounds in value when it's wired into your CRM and outbound queue.

## The B2B Sales Prospecting Playbook

Sales prospecting is the art of finding companies that could genuinely benefit from what you sell — before you spend a single minute on outreach. Done well, it fills your pipeline with accounts that match your ideal customer profile (ICP) and have the budget to buy. Done poorly, it burns your team's time on generic databases and cold calls that go nowhere.

The difference between the two is rarely effort. It's method. This playbook walks through the full prospecting workflow: defining who you're hunting for, finding the right accounts, qualifying them fast, and prioritizing the ones worth chasing.

## Step 1: Write Down Who You're Actually Selling To

You cannot prospect effectively until your ICP is specific enough to filter a market. A tight ICP answers these questions:

- **Industry** — which verticals feel your pain most acutely?
- **Company size** — employee count bands you can serve profitably
- **Location** — countries, states, or metro areas in your delivery zone
- **Technology** — tools they already use that signal a need
- **Behavioral signals** — hiring, expansion, funding, new locations

For example, an [SaaS company prospecting](/industries/saas) for new accounts might target software businesses with 10–50 employees that recently hired a sales rep — a strong signal they have a go-to-market motion and budget.

## Step 2: Build a Candidate Pool, Then Filter Hard

Most sales teams start with a database export and call it a list. The better approach is to build a broad candidate pool from multiple sources and then filter it ruthlessly against the ICP:

1. Business directories and industry registries
2. Job boards (hiring signals reveal growth)
3. Funding and news announcements
4. Google Maps and local business listings (excellent for SMB targets)
5. Social signals and company updates

Every company that survives this stage must match the ICP on at least your core firmographic filters. Cut duplicates, remove companies you already cover, and drop anything outside your size and location bands. A 50–70% reduction at this stage is normal and healthy.

## Step 3: Identify and Map the Buying Committee

Prospecting isn't about finding a company and sending one email to info@. It's about mapping who actually makes the decision:

- **Founder / CEO** — strategic purchases at smaller accounts
- **Sales Director / BDM** — sales tools and outreach platforms
- **Operations Manager** — process-heavy purchases
- **IT Director / CTO** — infrastructure, security, and managed services
- **Marketing Manager** — demand generation and content platforms

Include 2–4 targeted contacts per account, ranked by seniority and influence, rather than one guess per company.

## Step 4: Qualify Before You Contact

Qualification answers two questions: *Does this company have the problem you solve?* and *Can they afford the fix?*

Add a short qualification note to every account — the trigger that made it interesting, the budget signal you found, and the most relevant decision maker. When your reps open the list, they should know exactly why each account matters without re-researching it.

## Step 5: Prioritize With a Simple Scoring Model

Not all prospects are equal. Rank your list so reps work the best accounts first:

- **Fit score** — how well does the account match your ICP? (0–5)
- **Signal score** — how strong are the buying signals you found? (0–5)
- **Access score** — can you reach the right decision maker? (0–5)

Sort by total and work top-down. If you want a deeper framework for ranking prospects, our [B2B lead scoring guide](/blog/b2b-lead-scoring-guide) covers the model in detail.

## Step 6: Keep the Data Fresh and Verified

Prospecting data decays — roughly 2–3% of contacts go stale every month. Titles change, people move companies, and emails bounce. Before any campaign, verify:

- Email syntax and domain validity
- Mailbox status (valid, risky, or invalid)
- Current job titles and LinkedIn URLs

A verified list protects your domain reputation and your reply rates. ## Prospecting vs. Lead Generation

Sales prospecting is the *activity* of finding and researching accounts. B2B lead generation is the *system* that produces verified databases on demand — using research workflows to build prospect lists at scale. If prospecting is a firehose, lead generation is the filter that turns it into CRM-ready data.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many prospects should I target per month?**
It depends on deal size and sales cycle, but a healthy outbound motion typically works 500–1,500 qualified accounts per month at 2–4 contacts per account.

**How do I know if a company is a good prospect?**
Match it against your ICP firmographics and look for buying signals: recent hiring, expansion, new funding, or a tech stack that complements your product.

**Is cold calling dead?**
No — but it works best as part of a multi-channel approach that also includes personalized email and LinkedIn. The common denominator is good data.

**How fresh is prospect data?**
Quality lists are verified at build time and classified valid/risky/invalid so you know exactly what you're sending to.

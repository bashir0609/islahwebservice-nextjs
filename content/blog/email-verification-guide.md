## Email Verification: How to Cut Bounce Rates Before You Send

Email verification is the process of checking whether an email address actually exists and can receive mail — before you include it in a campaign. It is the difference between a campaign that lands in inboxes and one that destroys your domain reputation.

Most senders don't fail because their copy is weak. They fail because their list is dirty.

## Why Verification Matters So Much

Every email provider watches your sending reputation. Two numbers decide whether you land in the inbox or the spam folder:

- **Bounce rate** — the percentage of emails that fail to deliver
- **Complaint rate** — the percentage of recipients marking you as spam

Industry benchmarks say a bounce rate under 2% is healthy, under 5% is tolerable, and anything above that starts degrading your domain. A list full of invalid addresses is a self-inflicted reputation wound.

## How Verification Works

A solid verification pipeline runs layered checks:

1. **Syntax check** — does the address follow email format rules?
2. **Domain check** — does the domain exist and have valid MX records?
3. **Mailbox check** — does the specific inbox accept mail?
4. **Role and catch-all detection** — flags addresses like info@ or sales@ that route to shared inboxes

Each address is then classified into one of three states:

- **Valid** — passed all checks and is safe to send
- **Risky** — likely deliverable but with signals that could cause soft bounces or low engagement
- **Invalid** — will bounce; removed from the list

## The Cost of Skipping Verification

Let's make this concrete. A campaign to 10,000 addresses with a 10% invalid rate means 1,000 hard bounces. Providers like Google and Microsoft react to that within a few sends, not a few thousand. The result:

- Lower open rates on every future campaign
- Emails landing in spam even for warm prospects
- Rebuild the domain reputation over months

Verification at the list-building stage costs a fraction of what reputation repair costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good bounce rate for cold email?**
Under 2% is healthy. Above 5% starts damaging your domain reputation.

**What does "risky" mean in email verification?**
It means the address is likely deliverable but shows warning signals — such as a catch-all domain or a shared mailbox — that could cause soft bounces.

**Can you verify emails as part of list building?**
Yes. Verification is a standard stage in every list we build — every address is classified before delivery.

**How often should I verify my list?**
If you buy or source lists, verify at acquisition. If you maintain a database, re-verify before major campaigns — data decays by 2–3% per month.

## Recruitment Lead Generation: Researching Companies Around Hiring Signals

Recruitment and staffing firms have one research advantage most B2B sellers lack: the buying signal is public. Companies post jobs, publish hiring plans, and announce expansions — and all of it is checkable. Recruitment lead generation is the discipline of turning those public hiring signals into a criteria-matched prospect list and finding the people who own talent decisions.

This guide covers which hiring signals matter, how to combine them into a target profile, and which roles to research — with one important honesty rule at the end.

## Hiring Signals That Define a Recruitment Prospect

The strongest signals for staffing and recruitment outreach:

- **Active job postings** — volume of open roles on the company's own careers page, job boards, and LinkedIn
- **Hiring velocity** — how many roles opened over the last 30–90 days, not just what's open today
- **Department hiring** — whether growth is in engineering, sales, support, or operations (each implies a different staffing need)
- **Seniority of open roles** — manager and director openings signal team-building, not just backfill
- **Geographic expansion** — new office openings and hiring in new locations
- **Funding** — a recent round often funds a headcount plan
- **Employee growth** — net headcount change over 6–12 months
- **Repeat hiring patterns** — companies that hire in waves are recurring prospects

A company showing strong hiring velocity in departments you staff is a *criteria-matched account* — it matches the hiring criteria you agreed with the client. It is not a claim that the company definitely needs an agency.

## Turning Signals Into a Target Profile

Individual signals are noisy. A useful recruitment target combines several:

- **Sustained hiring** — open roles plus positive headcount growth over two quarters
- **Specific departments** — hiring in the verticals you specialize in
- **Size and stage** — scale-up companies (Series A–C, 50–500 employees) hire more predictably than either startups or enterprises
- **Geography** — markets where your agency has placement capability

The profile is a filter, agreed with the client in advance, applied to public data. The output is a list of companies that match — not a pipeline of guaranteed placements.

## Decision Makers in Talent Acquisition

The people responsible for hiring vary by company size. The roles worth researching:

- **HR Director / HR Manager** — owns hiring process and agency relationships at most companies
- **Talent Acquisition Manager / Recruitment Manager** — runs recruitment operations directly
- **Head of Talent / Head of People / People Director** — owns talent strategy at scale-ups
- **Department Head** — the hiring manager for department-specific roles
- **Founder / Owner** — the decision maker at small companies where HR is part-time

A researcher identifies who holds these roles and verifies the role is current. What a researcher cannot confirm is that a given person definitely approves staffing decisions or agency selection — ownership and process vary by company.

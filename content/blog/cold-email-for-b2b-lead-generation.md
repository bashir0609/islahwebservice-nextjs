## Cold Email for B2B Lead Generation

Cold email is one of the most cost-effective channels in B2B — when it's done right. When it's done wrong, it's a spam folder graveyard and a fast way to wreck your domain reputation.

The difference comes down to three things: the quality of the list you send to, the relevance of your message, and the health of your sending setup. This guide covers all three so your cold email campaigns move from first send to first reply.

## Why Most Cold Email Fails

Before we talk about what works, let's name what doesn't:

1. **Bad lists.** Sending to unverified or generic addresses means bounces and no replies.
2. **Blast messaging.** The same template to 5,000 unrelated companies reads as spam, because it is.
3. **Broken deliverability.** No SPF/DKIM setup, no warm-up, no reputation — and emails land in promotions or spam.

Every one of these is fixable. And the first fix is the one most teams skip: the list.

## Start With a Verified Prospect List

Your subject line and copy can be perfect, but if the email address is wrong, the campaign is dead on arrival. Cold email should only ever be sent to:

- **Validated email addresses** — syntax, domain, and mailbox-level checks
- **The right person** — the actual decision maker, not info@
- **Companies that match your ICP** — so the message is relevant

## The Anatomy of a Cold Email That Gets Replies

### 1. The Subject Line

Keep it short (30–50 characters), specific, and curiosity-driven. Mention something about *their* business, not yours:

- "Quick question about your sales hiring"
- "Your IT team just posted a new role"
- "Idea for [Company]'s onboarding"

Avoid spam triggers: ALL CAPS, "free", "guaranteed", and excessive exclamation marks.

### 2. The First Line Is About Them

Open with a specific, verifiable observation. It proves you did research and makes the email feel personal:

> "Saw that Acme just raised a seed round and is hiring three SDRs — congrats."

### 3. One Clear Value Proposition

State in one sentence how you help companies like theirs. Use a concrete outcome and a number if you have one:

> "We build verified prospect lists for B2B sales teams — the last 3 lists we shipped averaged under 1% bounce."

### 4. A Single, Low-Friction Call to Action

One ask, easy to say yes to: "Worth a 15-minute call this week?" Don't ask for a demo, a contract, or a meeting with your CEO. One step at a time.

## The Sequence Matters More Than the Email

Most replies come from follow-ups, not the first send. A standard high-performing sequence looks like:

1. **Day 0** — the initial email
2. **Day 3** — a short value-add follow-up (an insight, a resource, a relevant link)
3. **Day 7** — a brief check-in ("wanted to keep this on your radar")
4. **Day 14** — the polite breakup email

Keep each follow-up shorter than the last. If there's no reply after the fourth touch, stop — one or two messages can come from a colleague's inbox, then archive the account.

## Deliverability: The Unsexy Reason Campaigns Die

Great copy means nothing if your emails never reach the inbox. Protect your sending reputation:

- **Authenticate** — set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on your sending domain
- **Use a separate domain** — keep cold campaigns off your main corporate domain if you're sending at volume
- **Warm up** — gradually increase volume from a fresh domain
- **Keep bounce rates under 2%** — this is a direct consequence of list quality
- **Honor unsubscribes** — and include an unsubscribe link; it protects you and your domain

## Measure What Matters

Track these four numbers every campaign:

- **Bounce rate** — target under 2%
- **Open rate** — benchmark is 40–70% for cold email with good data
- **Reply rate** — 5–10% is strong for B2B
- **Positive reply rate** — the replies that actually matter: "let's talk"

If opens are low, the problem is deliverability or subject lines. If replies are low but opens are fine, the problem is copy or targeting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is cold email legal?**
In most regions yes, when you honor unsubscribe requests, identify yourself, and include a physical address. Check GDPR/CAN-SPAM rules for your targets.

**How many emails should I send per day?**
Start small (30–50 per day) on a warm domain and scale up gradually. Volume without reputation is just spam.

**What's a good reply rate for cold email?**
1–5% is typical; 5–10% is strong with verified data and relevant messaging.

**Why are my emails going to spam?**
Usually one of three things: unauthenticated sending, a new/unwarmed domain, or a list with too many invalid addresses.

## How to Research Recently Funded Companies

A company that just raised money is the most popular target in B2B prospecting — and for good reason. A fresh round often funds new hiring, new sales capacity, and new vendor evaluation. The problem is that "recently funded" is a fuzzy filter until you make it precise: funded *when*, at *what stage*, for *how much*, and visible in *which public sources*?

This guide explains how funding research actually works: the criteria that matter, where the data lives, how to verify it, and how to combine funding signals with other criteria to build a list worth calling.

## Define the Funding Criteria First

"Recently funded companies" is a research brief, not a researchable filter. Make it specific:

- **Funding date window** — last 3, 6, or 12 months? Date is the single most important variable; it determines how fresh the signal is
- **Funding stage** — seed, Series A, B, C, or growth rounds behave very differently
- **Round size** — a $2M seed round and a $50M Series C imply different budgets
- **Geography** — funding visibility varies by market; some regions report rounds less publicly
- **Industry and company size** — the round means different things for a 10-person startup and a 500-person scale-up

Once the window and stage are agreed, the research can be run. Until then, "recently funded" will return noise.

## Where Funding Data Lives

Funding information is scattered across public sources. A researcher typically checks:

- **Funding databases** — Crunchbase and similar platforms aggregate rounds, dates, and investors
- **Press releases and news** — most rounds above seed are announced via company press releases, TechCrunch, and industry press
- **Company announcements** — the company's own newsroom and investor pages
- **Registries and filings** — for markets and structures where filings are public
- **Investor announcements** — investor funds and partners often announce portfolio rounds

The researcher's job is to record the *date* of the round and the *stage* as reported — and to note when a round is rumored rather than confirmed.

## Verification Matters More Than Discovery

Funding data is a lagging indicator with a reputation problem: databases lag, press gets it wrong, and rounds are sometimes reported as "raised" when they're closed but not disbursed. For list quality:

- **Record the report date, not just the round date** — you want to know when the info was confirmed
- **Cross-check round size and stage** across at least two sources where possible
- **Confirm the company is still active** — funding history means nothing for a company that has wound down
- **Note secondary announcements** — the round that matters to your outreach is the one your prospect's team knows about

A verified funding record is a research signal, not a sales signal. A Series B raised three months ago means the company was expanding recently — it does not prove the company is evaluating vendors today.

## Combining Funding With Other Criteria

Funding is strongest as a *timing* filter layered on top of fit criteria:

- **Hiring** — funding plus a jump in open roles suggests the round is being put to work
- **Sales hiring** — funded companies hiring sales leadership and SDRs are often building outbound capacity
- **Tech stack** — funded companies re-evaluating their stack are visible through technology signals
- **Expansion** — new offices and new markets follow funding

The combination produces a criteria-matched list: companies that raised within your window, at your stage, and match your other filters.

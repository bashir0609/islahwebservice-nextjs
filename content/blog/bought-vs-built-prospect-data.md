## Bought vs. Built Prospect Data: What Each Approach Actually Delivers

Every sales team reaches the same fork: subscribe to a data platform and pull lists yourself, or commission researched, verified prospect data built around your criteria. The two approaches are not the same product at different prices — they're different products with different trade-offs. This guide compares them honestly, so you can pick the right one for the job.

## The Bought Option: Data Platforms and Subscriptions

Data subscriptions (LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, ZoomInfo-style platforms, and similar tools) give you access to large databases:

**What they're genuinely good at:**
- Self-serve exploration — you can filter, search, and pull records on demand
- Breadth — millions of records across markets and industries
- Speed — records are available immediately
- Ongoing access — re-query the database as your needs evolve

**The trade-offs:**
- **You do the work** — the platform gives you access, not a finished list; your team spends its hours filtering, exporting, and cleaning
- **Staleness is your problem** — database records carry the age of the database; verifying them is on you
- **Coverage gaps** — smaller, private, or niche companies are under-represented
- **Deliverability risk** — contact data from aggregated databases bounces at a rate you discover only when you send

A subscription is a tool. Like any tool, it's only as good as the person operating it and the data it contains.

## The Built Option: Commissioned Prospect Research

Commissioned research (what we do at Islah Web Service) delivers a finished, verified dataset built around your criteria:

**What it's genuinely good for:**
- **Criteria-matched lists** — research runs against your exact company and signal filters
- **Verification** — emails checked, roles confirmed, companies checked before delivery
- **Clean, CRM-ready output** — deduplicated, standardized, formatted for your workflow
- **Hard-to-find accounts** — niche verticals, specific tech stacks, private companies, and localized markets where databases are thin
- **Decision-maker focus** — research built around the roles you named, not just "contacts in industry X"

**The trade-offs:**
- **Lead time** — research takes days, not seconds
- **Cost per record** — higher than a database's marginal cost per pull
- **Point-in-time** — you get a verified snapshot, not an ongoing subscription

A researched list is a deliverable: built, verified, and finished. When you need a verified, criteria-matched list to act on, that's the point.

## Which One When

The honest answer is that they serve different jobs:

- **Exploration and scale** — when you're testing markets, need broad reach, or want self-serve access, a subscription is the right tool
- **Action and precision** — when you need a verified list for a specific campaign, a niche market, or hard-to-reach accounts, commissioned research wins
- **Hybrid** — many teams use a platform for exploration and commission research for the campaigns that matter

The failure mode to avoid is pretending they're interchangeable: pulling a platform export and calling it "researched" gives you neither the cost of a subscription nor the quality of research.

## What Researched Data Should Include

If you commission research, the deliverable should be explicit:

- The criteria and signals the list was built against
- Verification performed on every record, with dates
- Deduplication and standardization applied
- CRM-ready formatting with your custom columns
- Honest reporting of what could not be verified

## SaaS Prospecting: How to Research Companies That Match Your ICP

Selling software to software companies sounds easy — until you try to find the right ones at the right time. The SaaS market is crowded, and most prospecting lists fail because they filter on nothing more than "SaaS company" and "VP-level title."

This guide covers the research side of SaaS prospecting: how to define your ideal customer profile in researchable terms, which public signals separate a good target from a generic one, and how to identify the decision makers your sales team actually wants to reach.

## Start with Researchable ICP Criteria

Before any list is built, your ICP needs to be expressed as criteria a researcher can check against public data. Useful SaaS ICP filters include:

- **SaaS category** — the product category (analytics, security, HR tech, martech, vertical SaaS) and who it competes with
- **Company size** — headcount bands, which correlate loosely with team structure and buying process
- **Geography** — country, region, or city footprint
- **Funding stage and funding date** — a seed-stage company has a very different research profile from a Series C
- **Employee growth** — net headcount change over 6–12 months as a growth signal
- **Hiring activity** — open roles, especially in sales, marketing, and product
- **Technologies used** — CRM, marketing automation, data stack, and infrastructure choices
- **ARR or revenue where researchable** — public filings, funding rounds, and press disclosures
- **Expansion signals** — new offices, new markets, recent leadership hires

The point of criteria is match, not judgment. A company that matches your filters is a *criteria-matched account* — not a company you have proven needs your product.

## Growth Signals That Make a Company Researchable

For SaaS sellers, timing usually matters more than fit. These signals are public and checkable:

- **Recent funding** — a fresh round often funds new sales capacity. Funding date and stage are researchable via funding databases and press releases.
- **Sales hiring** — open sales rep, SDR, and sales leadership roles are a direct signal that a company is building outbound capacity.
- **Employee growth** — steady headcount growth over several quarters suggests expansion budgets.
- **New leadership** — a new VP of Sales, CRO, or Head of Growth often inherits a pipeline problem and is reachable before they've rebuilt their vendor list.
- **Marketing hiring** — growth-stage companies hiring marketing roles are often investing in demand generation.

These are research signals. They say "this company is expanding in ways worth investigating," not "this company is ready to buy."

## The Decision Makers SaaS Teams Want to Reach

For most SaaS outbound, the shortlist looks like:

- **VP Sales / Head of Sales / CRO** — accountable for revenue and usually the first stop for sales tooling
- **VP Marketing / Head of Growth** — owns demand generation, ABM, and the tech stack that powers it
- **RevOps** — owns the revenue tech stack, data quality, and CRM hygiene
- **IT leadership** — involved when the product touches infrastructure or security
- **Finance leadership** — relevant for larger deals and procurement-heavy categories

A researcher can identify who holds these roles at a target company and verify that they still hold them today. What a researcher cannot confirm is that any one person has budget authority or is the sole buyer — title research and purchasing authority are different questions.

## How the Research Actually Works

A focused SaaS prospecting project moves through six steps:

1. **Company research** — match companies against your ICP criteria (category, size, geography, funding, growth)
2. **Signal screening** — apply your requested indicators: funding date, hiring, tech stack, expansion
3. **Decision-maker discovery** — identify the roles above at each matched company
4. **Contact enrichment** — business email, LinkedIn, and company data for each person
5. **Verification** — email verification, role and company checks, deduplication, standardization
6. **Delivery** — CRM-ready records in CSV, Excel, or Google Sheets with your custom columns

If you already have a list, the same enrichment and verification steps can be applied to your existing database — you don't need to start from scratch.

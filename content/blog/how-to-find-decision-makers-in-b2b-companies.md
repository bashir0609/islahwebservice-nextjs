## How to Find Decision Makers in B2B Companies

"Find me the decision maker" is the most common request in B2B prospecting — and the least precise. A decision maker is not a title; it's a person with a role, a function, and a seniority level inside a specific company. The research job is to identify who holds the role you care about, confirm they still hold it, and deliver verified contact data for outreach.

This guide covers how that research actually works: translating your target role into researchable terms, mapping company structure, verifying what you find, and being honest about what public data cannot tell you.

## Translate the Role Before You Search

"Decision maker" means nothing to a researcher until it becomes a role. The translation usually happens along three axes:

- **Function** — sales, marketing, HR, finance, IT, operations
- **Seniority** — C-level, VP, director, manager, head of
- **Geography and company** — which companies, in which regions

So "decision maker for a recruitment agency selling to tech companies" becomes "HR Directors and Talent Acquisition Managers at Series B+ tech companies in Germany." Every extra axis shrinks the list and raises its quality.

## Research the Company Before the Person

Titles are only meaningful relative to company structure. Before identifying a person, a researcher checks:

- **Company size and stage** — a "Head of Sales" at a 20-person startup may be the founder; at a 500-person company they run a full team
- **Org structure** — product-led vs. sales-led companies organize revenue differently
- **Recent changes** — leadership moves, restructuring, or new offices change who holds which role
- **Subsidiaries and brands** — the person you want may sit in a subsidiary with a different name

Getting the company right first is what keeps the person research from being guesswork.

## Sources for Finding People

Once the role is defined, researchers pull from multiple public sources:

- **LinkedIn** — current title, function, and seniority, cross-checked against company pages
- **Company websites** — team pages, press releases, and about pages for executive roles
- **Org-chart data** — business data providers that aggregate roles and reporting lines
- **Business directories and registries** — for companies where leadership is registered publicly
- **Job postings** — a company hiring for a role tells you the role exists and who likely oversees it

A researcher records the current title and the date it was verified — both matter for outreach.

## Enrichment: What Gets Delivered

For each identified person, the deliverable typically includes:

- Full name and current role
- Business email (pattern-based or verified directly)
- LinkedIn URL
- Company, location, and company data
- Any custom fields you request

Enrichment fills in the data; verification confirms it. Email verification checks deliverability, role checks confirm the person still holds the title, and company checks confirm the account is still active.

## What Public Research Cannot Confirm

This is the part most buyers never hear: public data can tell you that someone *holds a role*. It cannot tell you whether that person:

- Has budget authority for your product
- Is the sole decision maker
- Is actively evaluating vendors
- Will respond to outreach

Anyone who guarantees those things is selling you something other than research. A verified current role at a criteria-matched company is a strong starting point for outreach — and it is not a promise of a meeting.

## Building a Decision-Maker List

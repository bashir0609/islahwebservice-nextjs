## How to Research Companies by Technology Stack

If you sell a CRM migration, a cybersecurity product, or a cloud tool, the most valuable filter you can apply to a prospect list is "companies that use the technology we replace or complement." Technology signals are public, checkable, and — unlike intent data — they describe what a company actually runs, not what someone speculates they want.

This guide explains which technology signals are researchable, where they come from, how to combine them with other criteria, and where the limits are.

## Which Technology Signals Are Researchable

The most reliable technology indicators for B2B prospecting:

- **CRM and sales tools** — which CRM a company runs (Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics, custom), marketing automation, and sales engagement platforms
- **Cloud and infrastructure** — AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, and database technologies
- **Cybersecurity indicators** — security vendors in use, where detectable from public footprints
- **Ecommerce and payments** — platforms, payment gateways, and cart technologies for online sellers
- **Product and development stack** — frameworks, hosting, and analytics tools visible from public infrastructure
- **Email and productivity** — Google Workspace vs. Microsoft 365, which matters for certain integrations

These signals come from technology-detection databases that scan public web footprints, DNS records, and client-side indicators, plus job postings that name the tools a company hires for.

## Combining Technology With Other Criteria

A technology signal on its own is a weak filter — half the market runs Salesforce. Technology research earns its place when combined with:

- **Company size** — the same CRM behaves differently at 50 employees and 5,000
- **Geography** — compliance and data-residency requirements vary by region
- **Industry** — a fintech running the same stack as a retailer is not the same account
- **Growth signals** — funding, hiring, and expansion indicate when a company might be re-evaluating its stack
- **Contract signals** — job postings for a new tool or a "migration engineer" can indicate upcoming change

The output is a *criteria-matched* list: companies that match your requested filters. It is not a list of companies proven to be looking for a new vendor.

## Where the Limits Are

Technology detection is powerful and imperfect. Be aware of:

- **Detection gaps** — not every company's footprint is fully visible; small and self-hosted environments are under-detected
- **Staleness** — stacks change; the detection date matters
- **Multi-vendor reality** — large companies run several tools in the same category, so "uses X" rarely means "uses only X"
- **No intent signal** — using a technology tells you what runs today, not whether the company is unhappy with it

A good research brief treats technology signals as filters, not as purchase intent.

## Using Tech-Stack Research in Practice

Three common use cases:

1. **Replacement plays** — a company running Tool A is the addressable market for Tool B. Research filters for Tool A users within your ICP.
2. **Integration plays** — a company running your platform plus a complementary tool is a natural expansion target.
3. **Compliance and fit plays** — cloud, security, or data-residency requirements narrow a broad market to companies whose stack already matches.

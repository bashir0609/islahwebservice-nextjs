## Challenge

A US SaaS company already had a contact database assembled over several years from event lists, web forms, and earlier campaigns. The project was not a request to discover a new market from scratch. It was an existing database enrichment assignment focused on improving records the client already owned.

The accumulated data contained missing business emails, outdated job titles, incomplete company information, and duplicate accounts. Those issues made the database harder to review and use consistently. Information collected at different times and through different channels also needed to be brought into a common structure before the client’s team could use it as a dependable working asset.

The challenge was therefore to preserve the useful records while filling supported gaps, updating fields that could be checked, and removing duplication. Enrichment could improve the completeness and consistency of the database, but it could not establish that every person remained interested in the SaaS product or that any record would produce a response, meeting, opportunity, or sale.

## Criteria

The starting point was the client’s existing database. Records were assessed for the specific deficiencies described in the project source: missing business emails, missing LinkedIn URLs, outdated job titles, incomplete company fields, and duplicate entries.

A record was eligible for enrichment because it was already part of the supplied data, not because it had been newly discovered as a prospect. The work needed to distinguish between a field that could be supported by current information, a field that remained unavailable, and a field that appeared outdated. Business email verification was required where an email could be found and where verification was available.

Company fields also needed to be completed and standardized. Duplicate handling applied at both the account and contact level because years of event, form, and campaign data could create multiple representations of the same organization or person. The final structure needed a verification date on every record, allowing the client to see when the enrichment work had been performed.

## Method

The enrichment process began by reviewing the supplied records and mapping their existing columns. This established which fields were present, which were missing, and where values needed normalization. The database could then be handled as a defined client-owned dataset rather than mixed with newly sourced companies.

For contact records, the research covered missing business emails and LinkedIn URLs, along with checks on current job titles. Where current public information supported a title update, the newer role information could replace an outdated value. Where a business email was available, it was added and passed through the available verification process. The wording “verified where available” is important because not every contact necessarily exposes a business email or yields the same level of verification evidence.

At company level, incomplete fields were researched and completed when support was available. Standardization brought comparable values into a consistent form so the records could be sorted, filtered, and imported more reliably. Deduplication then addressed repeated contacts and accounts without assuming that merely similar names represented the same entity.

A verification date was added to every record as stated in the original source. This did not make the data permanently current; it documented the point at which the record was reviewed. The completed file was prepared for the client’s CRM workflow, while campaign planning and execution stayed with the client.

## QA

Quality assurance covered completeness, currency, duplication, and formatting. Missing fields were reviewed to confirm whether supported information had been found or whether the field should remain unavailable. Updated job titles needed to correspond to the correct person and company rather than to a similarly named contact.

Email QA consisted of verification where business emails were available. ## Deliverable

The final deliverable was the client’s existing database in a cleaned, enriched, and CRM-ready format. It included business emails and LinkedIn URLs where those fields could be added, refreshed job-title information where current data supported an update, completed company fields, deduplicated records, and standardized formatting.

Each record carried a verification date, giving the client a clear reference point for the review. The database remained an enrichment deliverable rather than a newly built prospect list. That distinction preserves the nature of the engagement and avoids presenting existing client records as newly discovered accounts.

The deliverable did not include campaign strategy, outreach execution, appointment setting, or sales management. Those activities were handled entirely by the SaaS company’s own team.

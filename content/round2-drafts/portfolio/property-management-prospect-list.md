## Challenge

A commercial real estate software vendor needed prospect research centered on property ownership and management activity. Standard firmographic filters alone could not answer the core question. The requested accounts were companies that owned or managed multiple properties, so the research needed to connect organizations with observable portfolio information before identifying relevant contacts.

The target geography covered the vendor’s selected US metro areas. Within those markets, the project called for portfolio owners and operators, property-count information, office-footprint information, and contacts in property management, facilities, or operations leadership.

This made entity matching particularly important. A business name in a directory did not by itself demonstrate a multi-property portfolio, and a property record did not automatically identify the operating company or the right contact. The work needed to assemble a reviewable account profile without inferring that ownership or management activity proved interest in the vendor’s software.

## Criteria

The primary account criterion was that a company owned or managed multiple properties. This was a client-defined targeting condition based on portfolio activity rather than on industry and employee count alone. The project also applied the vendor’s target-metro scope.

Research fields included property counts and office footprints, using business directories and property records as described in the source. These fields represented the publicly observable portfolio information found during research. They did not certify a company’s internal software stack, procurement plans, operational problems, budget, or intention to change systems.

The contact criteria named director of property management, facilities director, and COO. The relevant person depended on each organization’s published structure. A title could support functional relevance, but it could not by itself prove final buying authority.

## Method

Research began with portfolio owners and operators in the target metro areas. Business directories and property records were used to investigate the relationship between an organization and its property footprint. The goal was to document the available property-count and office-footprint data rather than rely solely on a general company category.

Because property and company names can vary, the method required matching the observed portfolio information to the correct organization. The source does not document specific databases, matching thresholds, or exception rules, so none are added here. Where the relationship could be supported, the account record was enriched with the corresponding portfolio fields.

After account research, contact discovery focused on the director of property management, facilities director, and COO. Research considered the role structure actually available at each organization. Contact details were enriched where they could be found, while the account-level property information remained attached to the company record.

The data was prepared in a CRM-ready structure. Account information, portfolio fields, and contacts needed to remain correctly associated so the vendor could review why a company appeared in the database. Outreach and the later sales cycle were outside the scope and remained under the vendor’s control.

## QA

Account QA reviewed whether the researched organization could be connected to ownership or management of multiple properties in the target geography. Property-count and office-footprint fields were checked against the public directory or property-record context used during research.

Entity resolution was a central quality issue. Similar company names, operating brands, ownership entities, and local offices could create ambiguity. Records required review to avoid attaching a portfolio or contact to the wrong organization. The available source confirms the broad research approach but does not provide a measured match-accuracy rate: **METRIC-NEEDED**.

Contact QA checked whether each person held a current role aligned with property management, facilities, or operations leadership. Missing information was not to be converted into an assumed value. The final records were also reviewed for duplicates and standardized for CRM delivery.

No QA step treated portfolio size as evidence of immediate software demand. The project documented fit against the vendor’s criteria; it did not validate purchase intent, an active buying process, or readiness to replace an existing system.

Counts for accounts reviewed, accepted portfolios, mapped properties, verified contacts, and duplicates removed require retained evidence: **METRIC-NEEDED**.

## Deliverable

The vendor received a CRM-ready prospect database of criteria-matched portfolio owners and operators across its target metro areas. Company records included the researched property-count and office-footprint information described in the original source. Relevant contacts covered director of property management, facilities director, and COO functions according to the leadership information available for each account.

The deliverable connected the property-based reason for inclusion with the corresponding organization and contact records. This made it more specific than a generic real estate company list and allowed the vendor to work from documented account characteristics.

The project delivered research data only. The commercial real estate software vendor ran its own outreach and sales cycles. A final case study should add exact account, property, or contact totals only after evidence review; until then, those fields remain **METRIC-NEEDED**.

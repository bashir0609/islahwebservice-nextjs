## Challenge

A UK-based business intelligence SaaS company needed additional companies at the top of its funnel that matched its product-led growth targeting model. The assignment centered on prospect research and technographic segmentation rather than on running the company’s outreach.

The source describes a combined criteria set: companies using competing analytics stacks, companies showing recent hiring activity in data teams, and companies with Series A–C funding. It also identifies head of data, CTO, and analytics lead as the contact functions to research.

Combining these conditions required account-level evidence to stay distinct. A technology-stack observation, hiring activity, and funding stage are different research signals, and one should not be substituted for another. They could support fit against the client’s model, but they did not prove that a company intended to buy a new analytics platform, planned to replace a competitor, or had an active budget.

## Criteria

The account criteria covered the UK market and the client’s ideal customer profile. Technographic segmentation looked for companies running competing analytics stacks. Additional client-defined indicators included recent hiring in data teams and Series A–C funding.

These indicators were treated as observable characteristics requested by the client. A competing stack could explain the segment assigned to an account, while hiring or funding information could add further context. None of those facts independently established purchase intent or a guaranteed fit for the product beyond the stated research criteria.

Contact criteria focused on head of data, CTO, and analytics leads. Title variation was expected, so the research needed to match the function shown by the organization rather than assume every company used identical seniority labels.

The source does not state how recently hiring had to occur, which analytics technologies were included, whether every account needed all three indicators, or how many accounts and contacts were delivered. Those details cannot be invented. Quantitative totals and any unpublished threshold require documented confirmation: **METRIC-NEEDED**.

## Method

The work began by segmenting companies according to the technographic and business criteria in the brief. Publicly available information was reviewed for evidence of competing analytics stacks, recent data-team hiring, and Series A–C funding. Each signal belonged in its own field or segment so that the basis for inclusion remained understandable.

Once an account matched the applicable criteria, decision-maker research focused on data and technology leadership. The identified roles included head of data, CTO, and analytics lead. The person had to be connected to the correct company and hold a current role relevant to the client’s requested function.

The records were enriched with the available company and contact data. Contact details were verified where possible, and the completed dataset was deduplicated before formatting for the client’s CRM. This sequence separated account segmentation from contact enrichment and made it possible to review each stage independently.

The source uses “lead generation” in the project title, but the work described is prospect research and database delivery. Outreach sequences, meetings, and pipeline activity were entirely managed by the SaaS company’s own team.

## QA

Quality assurance first checked whether each account had evidence for the segment assigned to it. A technographic classification needed support from the researched stack information. Hiring and funding details needed to correspond to the correct company and to the project’s requested definitions where those definitions were supplied.

Contact QA reviewed the relationship among person, title, and account. A current data or technology leadership role was necessary for inclusion. Business contact data was verified where available, while unavailable details were not to be described as confirmed.

Deduplication covered repeated companies and contacts, and formatting QA ensured that account fields, segmentation evidence, and people data remained aligned in the CRM-ready output. The original source does not provide a verification rate, duplicate count, acceptance rate, or number of accounts in each segment. Each requires evidence: **METRIC-NEEDED**.

QA deliberately stopped short of interpreting competing-stack use, data hiring, or funding as intent. Those signals explained criteria matching; they did not establish that an account was seeking a replacement platform or prepared to enter a sales process.

## Deliverable

The client received a CRM-ready account and contact database organized around the requested technographic segments. Account records included the observable segmentation context available from competing analytics stacks, recent data-team hiring, and Series A–C funding. Relevant contacts included heads of data, CTOs, and analytics leads according to the organization’s published role structure.

The final data was enriched, verified where possible, and deduplicated. It provided the SaaS company with a structured prospecting input while leaving campaign decisions and execution under the company’s control.

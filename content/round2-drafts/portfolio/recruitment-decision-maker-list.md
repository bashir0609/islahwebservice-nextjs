## Challenge

A UK recruitment agency expanding into contract IT staffing needed a decision-maker list for enterprise organizations. Generic HR inboxes were not sufficient for the project. The agency wanted research tied to talent acquisition, IT hiring, and procurement functions so its own team could work with role-specific contact information.

The project required both account profiling and people research. Target organizations were assessed using tech hiring volume and contract spend, after which the relevant talent acquisition lead, IT hiring manager, and procurement contact had to be identified.

These criteria introduced an important evidence boundary. Hiring volume and contract-spend information could support inclusion where observable or otherwise supplied for the project. They did not prove that an organization had an immediate vacancy suitable for the agency, intended to add a staffing supplier, or had approved spend available to this agency. Likewise, a relevant title did not establish that one person held final authority over every staffing decision.

## Criteria

The account profile focused on UK enterprise organizations and used tech hiring volume and contract spend as the specified research criteria. The contact fields described in the source included role-specific emails and direct lines. The final database also needed to be deduplicated, standardized, and formatted for CRM use.

## Method

Research began with target-account profiling. Enterprise organizations were reviewed against the client’s tech-hiring-volume and contract-spend criteria using the information available for the project. This established the account context before named contacts were researched.

The people-research stage then covered talent acquisition, IT hiring, and procurement. The target titles were talent acquisition lead, IT hiring manager, and procurement contact, but the exact wording could differ among organizations. Research needed to connect a current person and function to the correct account without assuming authority beyond what the role evidence supported.

Where available, role-specific business emails and direct lines were captured. These details were associated with the named contact rather than substituted with a generic HR inbox. The source does not identify the research platforms, phone-validation method, email-verification process, or confidence categories, so none are introduced in this draft.

The records were deduplicated and standardized before CRM-ready delivery. This included aligning account and contact fields and ensuring that talent, IT hiring, and procurement roles remained distinguishable. Outreach and placement activity stayed entirely with the recruitment agency.

Contact QA checked that each person was currently associated with the correct organization and held a role relevant to the requested function. Role-specific email and direct-line fields were reviewed where available. ## Deliverable

The recruitment agency received a CRM-ready decision-maker database covering criteria-matched UK enterprise organizations. Contact records included talent acquisition leads, IT hiring managers, and procurement contacts according to the roles that could be identified at each account.

The final dataset contained role-specific emails and direct lines as stated in the original source, with records deduplicated and standardized. It gave the agency a structured research asset that was more specific than a collection of general HR addresses.

The deliverable did not include managed outreach, candidate placement, appointment setting, or campaign execution. Those activities were handled entirely by the agency’s team.

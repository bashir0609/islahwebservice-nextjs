## Challenge

A US professional services consultancy had accumulated a legacy CRM containing outdated contacts, incomplete records, and gaps in decision-maker data. The database already existed, so the assignment was not to discover an entirely new market or operate an outreach campaign. It was an existing-database enrichment project: review the consultancy’s records, update the contact and company information supported by current research, and make the resulting segments more usable by the consultancy’s own marketing team.

Legacy CRM data creates several practical problems at once. A person may still appear under a company after leaving it, a title may no longer reflect the person’s current role, and important contact fields may be missing. Even where a record remains directionally useful, inconsistent formatting can make segmentation and account-level review difficult. The consultancy therefore needed more than a set of newly appended email addresses. It needed a structured way to distinguish current contacts from departed contacts and to reconnect accounts with the roles relevant to its current targeting.

## Criteria

The working criteria were tied to the enrichment fields and account-remapping requirement documented for the project. Each usable record needed current job-title information, a business email verified where available, a direct dial where available, and company technographic data. Records also needed a status that allowed departed contacts to be flagged for removal rather than silently retained as though they were current.

At the account level, the task required mapping companies back to their current decision-maker roles. This is an important distinction: a previously known person is not automatically the right current contact, and a role label alone does not prove final purchasing authority. The defensible objective was to identify and document current role alignment according to the consultancy’s targeting needs, not to claim that any researched person had budget, intent, or guaranteed authority.

The database also needed enough consistency for the consultancy’s team to work with enriched segments. That meant treating field definitions and record status consistently across the file. ## Method

The work began from the consultancy’s existing CRM rather than from a blank prospect list. Each record could then be considered in the context of its known company, known contact, available fields, and current status. Research focused on whether the contact still matched the account and whether the documented job title remained current.

Where the evidence showed that a contact had departed, the record was flagged for removal. Flagging preserved a clear distinction between an unusable legacy contact and a currently mapped person. Where account coverage needed to be refreshed, the account was re-mapped to the current decision-maker roles specified for the project. Current job titles were added or updated, and the available business contact information was enriched.

The process also covered verified emails, direct dials, and company technographics. “Verified” here describes the email checks available during the research workflow; it should not be expanded into a promise of permanent deliverability. Direct dials and technographic fields likewise depended on what could be supported for an individual record. Missing information should remain missing or be marked according to the project’s conventions rather than filled through assumption.

Finally, enriched records were organized into usable segments. This supported the consultancy’s internal campaign planning while keeping the service boundary clear: Islah built and improved the data; the consultancy’s own marketing team controlled campaign planning and execution.

## QA

Quality assurance centered on recency, traceability, and consistency. Current titles had to correspond to the researched contact rather than being copied from an old CRM value without review. Departed contacts needed an explicit removal flag so they did not remain mixed with current contacts. Account remapping required checking that the replacement role belonged to the relevant company and matched the project’s role criteria.

Contact fields also required field-level review. Emails described as verified needed to have passed the verification checks available at the time. Direct dials and technographics needed to stay attached to the correct person or company. Standardized field handling reduced ambiguity when the consultancy filtered or imported the enriched segments.

## Deliverable

The deliverable was the enriched legacy CRM, organized into segments and updated with current job titles, verified business emails where available, direct dials where available, and company technographics. Departed contacts were flagged for removal, and accounts were re-mapped to current decision-maker roles where the legacy contact no longer provided suitable coverage.

This was a data deliverable rather than an outreach service. It gave the consultancy a clearer operational distinction between current records, records requiring removal, and accounts with refreshed role coverage.

## Challenge

A US cybersecurity managed service provider wanted to launch cold email outreach to mid-market manufacturing and healthcare companies. Those two target segments were selected in the context of strict compliance needs, but the MSP needed the underlying account and contact data before its team could run outreach. The project therefore focused on prospect research and list preparation, not on writing sequences, sending messages, managing replies, or booking meetings.

The data challenge had multiple layers. The MSP needed companies within its selected mid-market segments, the relevant roles at each company, and contact details that had been checked before delivery. Generic company inboxes would not satisfy the brief because the requested contacts were the IT security leader and compliance officer. Role-based and catch-all addresses also needed to be removed before the file reached the client.

## Criteria

The target-account criteria were centered on US mid-market manufacturing and healthcare companies. At each included company, the requested contact coverage consisted of the IT security leader and compliance officer. These titles describe the functions sought during research; they do not establish that every organization used identical title wording or that every person had sole purchasing authority.

Email suitability formed a separate set of criteria. The project called for real-time inbox verification and excluded role-based and catch-all addresses. A record needed to be connected to the relevant person and company, pass the checks available during research, and survive the exclusion process before delivery. The requested result was a working research dataset for the MSP’s team, not a guarantee that every mailbox would remain deliverable after the research date.

The final file also needed to be deduplicated, standardized, and CRM-ready. That required consistent field treatment so accounts and contacts could be imported and used without avoidable cleanup. ## Method

Research began with companies matching the client-defined segment criteria. Manufacturing and healthcare were handled as explicit target categories rather than as evidence that an individual company had active buying intent. Compliance needs provided context for the MSP’s targeting strategy, but they did not prove that a researched company was seeking a cybersecurity provider.

For each selected company, research then focused on the IT security and compliance functions. The objective was to identify the relevant leader or officer using the title and organizational information available for that account. Where title variations existed, the underlying functional match mattered more than forcing every person into one exact title label.

Business emails associated with those contacts were validated with real-time inbox verification. Role-based mailboxes and catch-all addresses were removed before delivery, consistent with the original source. Records were then reviewed for duplicate entries and standardized so that equivalent values did not appear in conflicting formats. This sequence kept account selection, role identification, email checking, exclusion, and formatting as distinct stages rather than treating a found email as a finished record.

The resulting file was prepared for the client’s CRM. Islah’s work ended with the researched data deliverable. The MSP retained control over message strategy, sending infrastructure, campaign operation, follow-up, and sales conversations.

## QA

Quality assurance addressed both record identity and email status. Company and contact relationships needed to remain intact through the workflow so an otherwise valid email was not attached to the wrong account. Role matching needed to reflect either the IT security or compliance function requested by the MSP. Duplicate review helped prevent the same account or person from appearing more than once under minor formatting variations.

Email QA included the documented real-time inbox verification step and the removal of role-based and catch-all addresses. That is a scoped process statement, not a promise of zero bounces. Mailbox conditions can change, and the source offers no measured post-delivery campaign data. A reviewed case study should therefore avoid converting “inbox-checked” into a permanent guarantee.

## Challenge

A US-based managed IT services provider needed a prospect database for its managed cybersecurity retainer program. The assignment required more than a broad directory of small and midsize businesses. Companies had to fit a defined employee-size band, operate in the provider’s target metro areas, and have relevant leadership contacts who could be researched and verified.

The original brief identified companies with 50–500 employees and named IT director, vCISO, and operations leadership as the contact functions of interest. The challenge was to connect account-level fit with current people-level information while keeping the database useful for the provider’s own campaign workflow.

An account’s size, location, or leadership structure could establish a match against the client’s requested criteria. Those observable facts did not establish an active cybersecurity need, dissatisfaction with an incumbent provider, available budget, purchase intent, or readiness to enter a retainer. The research therefore focused on criteria matching and contact verification rather than unsupported sales qualification.

## Criteria

At account level, the provider requested US companies with 50–500 employees across specified target metro areas. The employee band was a client-defined targeting condition and should be presented as such, not as evidence that every organization had the same security requirements.

At contact level, the requested functions were IT director, vCISO, and operations leadership. Because organizational structures differ, not every target company would necessarily publish every title. The relevant record needed to identify an applicable person based on the available role structure rather than force a title onto an account where it did not exist.

The project also called for validated business emails and direct dials, with the completed records deduplicated and standardized for CRM delivery. The current portfolio source contains an exact total for verified decision-maker contacts, but publication of a quantitative delivery claim requires retained project evidence. Until that evidence is attached to the case-study review, the draft uses **METRIC-NEEDED** instead of treating the number as independently substantiated.

Criteria matching was not intent scoring. No company was to be labeled ready to buy solely because it fit the employee range, operated in a target metro, or had a relevant leader.

## Method

Research began with account discovery inside the requested metro areas. Company information was assessed against the 50–500 employee criterion, and records that matched the defined account scope moved to contact research.

The contact stage focused on identifying current IT director, vCISO, and operations leadership roles. The research had to account for differences in title naming and organizational design. A functionally relevant leader could be recorded where the role aligned with the brief, but the data did not claim that a title automatically conferred final purchasing authority.

Business contact details were enriched after the person and role were matched to the correct organization. The original source states that contacts went through multi-channel validation and that direct dials and validated emails were included. The specific channels, verification tools, status categories, and acceptance rules are not documented in the source, so the draft does not add them.

The working data was then deduplicated and standardized. This reduced repeated account or contact entries and aligned the fields for CRM import. The process was designed to produce a structured research asset for the provider, not to run the provider’s campaign or make claims about the performance of that campaign.

## QA

Account QA checked the stated employee band and geography against the information available during research. Where company size information varied across sources, the record needed review rather than an unsupported assumption. Metro-area matching likewise needed to follow the client’s geographic definition.

Contact QA checked the relationship between person, role, and company. Current-role evidence was important because leadership records can become outdated. Email and phone fields were validated as described in the source, while the exact validation methodology remains undocumented and should not be expanded beyond what is known.

Deduplication reviewed repeated companies and people, and standardization checked that comparable data appeared in consistent columns and formats. A publishable count of accepted records, rejected records, validated emails, direct dials, duplicates removed, or field-level accuracy requires evidence: **METRIC-NEEDED**.

QA did not evaluate whether a company had a current cybersecurity project, had experienced a security incident, intended to replace a provider, or possessed an approved retainer budget. None of those conclusions follows from the criteria in the available source.

## Deliverable

The managed IT provider received a CRM-ready prospect database covering criteria-matched companies in its target metro areas and relevant IT, vCISO, and operations leadership contacts. The records included validated emails and direct dials as stated in the original project description, and the dataset was deduplicated and standardized for the client’s use.

The source describes the final list as containing an exact number of verified contacts. Because the requested draft standard requires quantitative proof, that total remains represented as **METRIC-NEEDED** pending a supporting export, acceptance record, or other project evidence.

The deliverable was the data itself. Campaign execution and campaign performance remained entirely with the client. No managed cold email, LinkedIn outreach, appointment setting, or sales closing formed part of the work described.

## Outcome Type

The supported outcome was a structured prospect database aligned with the provider’s account and role criteria. It gave the client a researched set of companies and contacts that could be loaded into its CRM and used in its own managed cybersecurity campaign planning.

This was a data-delivery outcome, not a guaranteed pipeline outcome. The existing source does not provide verified response rates, meetings, opportunities, retainer agreements, pipeline value, or revenue. Any such quantitative or commercial outcome requires separate evidence and should remain **METRIC-NEEDED** unless the client supplies and approves it.

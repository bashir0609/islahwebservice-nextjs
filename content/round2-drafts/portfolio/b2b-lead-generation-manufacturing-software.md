## Challenge

A US manufacturing software vendor needed a repeatable way to identify plants and production facilities that matched the adoption criteria for its automation product. A broad list of manufacturing companies would not provide the facility-level detail the client required. The research needed to distinguish criteria-matched plants and then map the relevant leadership at each facility.

The assignment combined a target model with recurring research. The model used plant size, industry codes, and installed-equipment signals. Contact research focused on the plant manager, vice president of operations, and engineering lead. The resulting database needed verified contact details, deduplication, standardization, and CRM-ready delivery.

The source states that the vendor now uses the same targeting framework quarterly to refresh its lists. It does not provide the number of facilities, the plant-size bands, the industry codes, the specific equipment signals, or the number of quarterly refreshes completed. ## Criteria

The target-account model was built from three documented dimensions: plant size, industry codes, and installed-equipment signals. Together, these represented the vendor’s client-defined adoption criteria. A facility needed to match the specified model before it qualified for inclusion in the account database.

These characteristics were observable targeting inputs, not proof that a plant intended to purchase automation software. Installed equipment, company classification, and facility size can support criteria matching, but they do not establish budget, urgency, or readiness to switch systems.

At the contact level, the requested roles were plant manager, VP of operations, and engineering lead. These covered plant, operations, and engineering leadership. The source does not say that every facility had all three titles or that the same title conventions applied across every account.

## Method

The work began by translating the vendor’s criteria into a repeatable firmographic target model. Plant size, industry codes, and installed-equipment signals were kept as separate research dimensions so each facility could be assessed consistently. The purpose was to make inclusion explainable rather than assemble a list based on a broad manufacturing label.

Research then identified plants and production facilities matching the model. Because the brief was facility-oriented, the workflow needed to maintain the relationship between the plant and its parent or operating company where relevant without replacing the facility-level target with generic corporate data.

For each selected facility, contact research focused on the plant manager, VP of operations, and engineering lead. Available business contact details were researched and verified, then linked to the correct facility and role. The database was reviewed for duplicates and standardized into a consistent format for the client’s CRM.

The targeting framework was reusable, allowing the vendor to apply the same criteria during quarterly list refreshes. That repeatability concerns the research method; it does not imply that the same accounts or contacts remained unchanged each quarter. Campaign execution and sales activity stayed with the vendor’s team.

## QA

QA started with facility-level criteria matching. Plant size, industry codes, and installed-equipment signals needed to refer to the same target facility. This reduced the risk that a valid attribute from one location would be applied to another location or to a parent company without support.

Contact QA required a correct relationship among facility, company, person, and role. The plant manager, operations leader, and engineering lead were reviewed as separate functional targets. A person’s title did not by itself prove final decision authority or active purchase intent.

Verification, deduplication, and standardization formed the final data-quality layer.

## Challenge

A US fintech software company selling compliance and payments tools needed a fresh prospect list for its outbound team. Rather than requesting a broad directory of companies, it defined the project around funding-stage criteria: companies that had raised within a specified window. The assignment was to turn those filters into a criteria-matched account and contact database that the client could use as an outbound starting point.

The research challenge was not simply to locate funded companies. Funding stage and last-funding date needed to be documented for every delivered record, and the client also requested observable signals related to hiring in compliance, engineering, and finance. Relevant finance, compliance, and technical contacts then needed to be identified at each account.

These signals required careful interpretation. A funding event or hiring activity can be documented as a research fact when supported by a source, but neither proves that a company needs the client’s software, has an approved budget, or intends to buy. The list was designed around client-defined filters, not a claim of purchase intent.

## Method

The work started with account research against the client’s stated funding criteria. Companies were reviewed for stage and last-funding date, and only criteria-matched accounts were suitable for the resulting database. The relevant funding details were retained as record-level fields so the client could see why an account met the filter rather than receiving an unexplained list.

The requested hiring signals were then researched across compliance, engineering, and finance. These indicators added client-defined context to each account. Their use remained descriptive: they showed that a company matched the requested observable signal, not that the company had expressed interest in the client’s compliance or payments tools.

Once an account met the criteria, decision-maker research focused on the CFO, VP Finance, Head of Compliance, and CTO. The role set crossed financial, regulatory, and technical functions, giving the client structured contact coverage aligned with its own outbound plan. Contact findings were linked back to the correct company and role.

Before delivery, the database was deduplicated and formatted for the client’s CRM. The fintech company’s team retained responsibility for sequence design, outreach, qualification, and sales activity. The research process supplied the data foundation but did not operate the campaign.

## QA

QA began with criteria matching. Funding stage and last-funding date needed to correspond to the same account and appear on the delivered record. Hiring signals needed to reflect the requested functions and remain associated with the correct company. This reduced the risk of presenting a company as criteria-matched merely because one disconnected data point appeared relevant.

Contact QA required checking company affiliation and role alignment for the CFO, VP Finance, Head of Compliance, and CTO findings. A title was treated as an observable role, not proof that the person was the final buyer. Duplicate checks then addressed repeated companies or contacts, including entries that might differ only in formatting.

The final structure was reviewed for CRM readiness, including consistent fields for funding information and contact roles.

# B2B Prospect Research for Real Estate and Property Markets

Real estate and property research often involves several connected entities: properties, ownership companies, operators, management firms, portfolios, and individual professionals. Islah Web Service researches these records against client-defined criteria and organizes the findings into a structured dataset for the client’s own workflow.

The service is focused on research, enrichment, cleaning, and formatting. Islah does not conduct outreach, broker transactions, book meetings, or guarantee opportunities. **We Build the Data. Your Team Controls the Outreach.**

## Define the entity and property criteria

A useful real estate brief begins by stating what the primary record represents. It may be a property, ownership entity, operating company, management company, investor, developer, or professional contact. Mixing these levels without identifiers can create duplicates and misleading relationships.

The brief should define included property types, locations, portfolio characteristics, ownership rules, and relevant roles. It should also explain how subsidiaries, special-purpose entities, joint ownership, management relationships, and multiple properties under one organization should be handled.

Public property and company information varies by jurisdiction and source. Some requested fields may not be available or may require careful reconciliation. Uncertain relationships should be flagged rather than stated as established facts.

## Example ICP research criteria

ICP here means transparent, client-defined targeting criteria. Matching them does not establish motivation to sell, buy, refinance, change vendors, or engage with the client.

| Criteria area | Example definition | Research treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Primary entity | Property, owner, investor, developer, operator, or manager | Keep entity types separate and linked with identifiers |
| Property type | Multifamily, office, industrial, retail, hospitality, land, or another class | Use the client’s classification and flag mixed-use cases |
| Geography | Property location, owner headquarters, or target operating market | Apply the specified location basis consistently |
| Ownership | Direct owner, parent entity, related entity, or reported beneficial relationship | State only relationships supported by available evidence |
| Portfolio criteria | Property count, property type mix, or defined portfolio range | Record where researchable without inventing completeness |
| Property characteristics | Unit count, square footage, class, year, or another requested field | Include source-dependent fields with appropriate notes |
| Management relationship | Self-managed or named third-party manager | Record only when supported and distinguish from ownership |
| Target roles | Acquisitions, asset management, property management, development, operations, or executive roles | Apply an agreed role hierarchy without claiming final authority |
| Exclusions | Property classes, geographies, owner types, current accounts, or named entities | Apply consistently and flag borderline records |

Criteria such as portfolio size or property count need a defined source and interpretation. A visible subset of properties may not represent an owner’s complete portfolio. The output should avoid claiming completeness unless evidence supports it.

## Property, company, and professional research

A prospect-research project can begin with properties and work toward ownership and relevant people, or begin with companies and map selected property or portfolio information. The correct direction depends on the client’s purpose and available identifiers.

Contact research can focus on acquisitions, asset management, property management, development, operations, facilities, or executive roles. The presence of a role indicates professional relevance under the agreed criteria; it does not prove that the person controls a particular decision or that the organization has a current requirement.

For supplied property or owner records, enrichment can complete missing fields, standardize addresses and entity names, add company domains or professional profiles, research business emails where available, and flag potential duplicate entities.

## Sample fields

A real estate and property dataset may include:

- Property name and standardized address
- City, region, postal code, and country
- Property type and subtype
- Requested property characteristics where available
- Ownership entity name
- Parent, related, or operating company where supported
- Company website and domain
- Property-management company where supported
- Portfolio or property-count field with source notes where requested
- Contact name
- Current title and functional area
- Public professional profile or company biography URL
- Business email and verification status where available
- Property, ownership, company, and contact source URLs
- Entity relationship notes
- Criteria-match, uncertainty, conflict, or review status
- Client property ID, account ID, or CRM key

Entity IDs are especially important when names repeat or one company is connected with several properties. The delivery structure should allow clients to distinguish property records from organization and person records.

## Prospect research versus database enrichment

Choose B2B Prospect Research when you need new properties, ownership entities, property companies, or relevant professionals discovered from defined criteria. This may include company discovery, property-to-owner research, account research, or role research.

Choose Existing Database Enrichment when you already have property, company, or contact records and need missing ownership details, websites, titles, profiles, business emails, standardized names, normalized addresses, or duplicate review.

A combined scope should identify the source of each record and preserve client-supplied property or account IDs. Newly discovered entities should not be silently merged into existing records when the relationship is uncertain.

## Building a reviewable dataset

The project starts with entity definitions, criteria, fields, geography, exclusions, relationship rules, and delivery format. Terms such as “owner,” “operator,” “manager,” and “portfolio” should be defined because sources may use them differently. The client should state whether historical ownership is relevant and which date basis should apply.

A sample can test the research path and reveal difficult cases: special-purpose entities, shared addresses, recently sold properties, mixed-use assets, multiple management companies, parent-subsidiary structures, and professionals with responsibilities across affiliated firms. These cases can then receive consistent status labels.

During production, addresses, entity names, property classes, titles, and locations can be standardized. Potential duplicates can be flagged using agreed matching rules. Sources and notes can accompany fields where relationships are complex. The final spreadsheet or CSV can be mapped to the client’s desired CRM or database columns.

Research reflects information available at the time. Property ownership and professional roles can change, and public records may update on different schedules. Date and source context helps the client decide whether additional verification is needed.

## Evidence boundaries in property research

An ownership record, permit, portfolio characteristic, job posting, leadership change, or management relationship can be useful research information. It does not automatically demonstrate intent to sell, acquire, refinance, hire a vendor, or change a service provider. Client-defined signals should be labeled as signals rather than conclusions.

Relationships should also be expressed carefully. A mailing address, shared officer, similar name, or website reference may warrant review but may not by itself establish ownership or control. Where sources conflict, the output should preserve that uncertainty.

Business email verification may be included where available. It does not guarantee delivery, a response, a meeting, access to a property owner, or a commercial transaction. The client remains responsible for outreach and its use of the data.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you research property owners?

Yes, when ownership research is part of a clearly defined scope and suitable information is available. The output should distinguish direct, parent, related, and uncertain entities rather than treating every association as ownership.

### Can you build lists by property type and location?

Yes. Define the property class, mixed-use rules, geographic basis, required characteristics, exclusions, and primary record type. Availability varies by field and jurisdiction.

### Can you identify acquisitions or asset-management contacts?

Yes. Provide preferred functions, titles, seniority, and fallback roles. A relevant title does not prove authority over a particular property or transaction.

### Can you enrich our existing property database?

Yes. Existing Database Enrichment can complete requested property, owner, company, and contact fields; standardize names and addresses; add profiles; verify business emails where available; and flag potential duplicates.

### Do you identify owners ready to sell or buy?

No. Islah researches observable information and client-defined signals. Motivation, intent, budget, and transaction readiness should not be inferred as established facts.

### Do you contact owners or arrange meetings?

No. Islah delivers research data. Your team controls outreach, relationship building, due diligence, and transaction activity.

# B2B Prospect Research for Recruitment and Staffing Firms

Recruitment and staffing firms need prospect data that reflects the markets, employer types, departments, and roles they actually serve. Islah Web Service researches companies and relevant professional contacts against a defined brief, then organizes the findings into a reviewable, CRM-ready dataset.

The service stops at research and data delivery. Islah does not run cold email, send LinkedIn messages, provide outsourced recruiting outreach, book meetings, or guarantee client acquisition. **We Build the Data. Your Team Controls the Outreach.**

## Research based on a clear employer profile

A broad list of companies that are “hiring” is rarely specific enough. The brief should define which industries, locations, company sizes, departments, and vacancy types are relevant. It should also explain whether hiring activity is required, preferred, or simply one requested field.

Hiring information is a client-defined research signal. An open role can be observed and recorded, but it does not prove that an employer wants agency support, has external recruitment budget, or will engage a staffing firm. The dataset should preserve that distinction.

Projects can focus on new employer discovery, contact research within supplied accounts, or enrichment of an existing business-development database. Candidate sourcing is not implied by this page; the deliverable described here is B2B company and professional research for the client’s own workflow.

## Example ICP research criteria

ICP here means the client’s transparent targeting rules, not an assertion of intent or qualification.

| Criteria area | Example definition | Research treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Employer industry | Named sectors, subsectors, and exclusions | Classify against the agreed taxonomy |
| Geography | Headquarters, hiring location, office, or operating market | Keep the selected location meaning consistent |
| Company size | Employee band or another agreed measure | Record where available without inventing precision |
| Hiring activity | Open roles, departments, locations, or date window | Capture observable postings and observation date |
| Vacancy relevance | Job families, seniority, employment type, or skill area | Match only the definitions in the brief |
| Department | Technology, finance, healthcare, operations, or another function | Use for segmentation where researchable |
| Target roles | Talent acquisition, HR, people, department, operations, or executive roles | Apply a client-approved hierarchy and fallbacks |
| Organization type | Corporate employer, portfolio company, nonprofit, or another type | Include only categories supported by sources |
| Exclusions | Current clients, agencies, public bodies, locations, or named companies | Apply before delivery and flag ambiguous records |

Role rules may vary by company size. A smaller employer might use a founder or operations title, while a larger one may have talent acquisition leadership. The brief should specify acceptable alternatives instead of assuming that every company uses the same organizational structure.

## Employer and contact research

Company discovery can identify employers that match the defined industry, location, size, and other requirements. Hiring activity can be added when requested, along with the role category, location, and observation date. Contact research can identify relevant HR, talent, people, departmental, operations, or executive roles according to the agreed hierarchy.

A title should be recorded as an observable fact supported by available information. Describing someone as the “decision-maker” should be used carefully because title alone may not prove final authority. The client can decide how to prioritize or validate contacts within its own sales process.

## Sample fields

A recruitment or staffing prospect dataset may include:

- Employer name, website, and domain
- Industry and employer type
- Headquarters or agreed operating location
- Employee-size band where available
- Hiring activity status and observation date
- Open-role title, department, location, and source URL where requested
- Vacancy category or client-defined relevance label
- Contact name
- Current title and department
- Seniority based on the agreed taxonomy
- Public professional profile URL
- Business email and verification status where available
- Company and contact source URLs
- Criteria-match notes
- Exclusion, uncertainty, or manual-review status
- Client account ID, territory, owner, or CRM key

A project does not need every possible field. Selecting fields that support an actual workflow helps reduce unnecessary research and makes quality review clearer.

## Research process and quality controls

The project begins by defining the market, vacancy rules, role hierarchy, fields, exclusions, and delivery format. Terms such as “actively hiring,” “technology role,” or “senior vacancy” should have practical definitions. A date window may be needed because a role observed months ago may no longer be open.

A sample can test whether employer types, vacancy categories, and contacts match the brief. It can also show how repeated vacancies, staffing agencies, confidential postings, remote roles, subsidiaries, and multi-location employers will be treated.

During production, values can be standardized across company names, industries, locations, departments, titles, and statuses. Duplicate or likely duplicate records can be flagged. Conflicting sources should be noted when they affect a required field. Unavailable data should remain visibly unavailable rather than being filled with an assumption.

Delivery can use the client’s preferred spreadsheet or CSV columns. Before CRM import, the client should review field mapping, duplicate rules, and whether current account ownership or suppression lists have been applied correctly.

## What the data can and cannot show

The research can show that an employer matched the agreed observable criteria at the time reviewed. It can record a publicly visible vacancy, a company characteristic, or a current professional title. It cannot establish undisclosed recruitment budget, dissatisfaction with an internal team, willingness to use an agency, or intent to buy.

Job postings change quickly and may be duplicated across sources. Some are evergreen, already filled, reposted, or managed by a third party. A clear source and observation date help the client interpret the record. The client remains responsible for deciding whether and how to approach an organization.

Business emails may be researched and verified where available. Verification does not guarantee deliverability, a response, a meeting, a vacancy instruction, or a placement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you find employers currently advertising relevant roles?

Yes, when hiring activity is part of the scope. Define role families, locations, date window, and exclusions. The observation is a research signal, not proof that the employer wants agency support.

### Can you identify HR and talent acquisition contacts?

Yes. The brief can specify preferred titles, seniority, departments, and fallback roles. A relevant title does not by itself establish final authority.

### Can you research contacts for a list of target employers?

Yes. Supply the account list and stable identifiers, then define required roles and fields. This is prospect research focused on people within named accounts.

### Can you clean an old recruitment CRM export?

Yes. Existing Database Enrichment can review titles, complete requested fields, add profiles, verify business emails where available, standardize values, and flag likely duplicates.

### Do you source candidates or contact employers?

This service page describes B2B employer and professional research. Islah does not manage outreach, book meetings, or promise placements through this service.

### Do you guarantee that hiring companies will become clients?

No. Matching criteria or showing hiring activity does not guarantee interest, replies, meetings, instructions, placements, or revenue.

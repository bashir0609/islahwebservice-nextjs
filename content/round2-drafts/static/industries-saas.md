# B2B Prospect Research for SaaS and Technology Companies

SaaS and technology teams often need prospect data organized around a narrow market definition rather than a broad list of “software companies.” Islah Web Service researches companies and relevant professional roles against client-defined criteria, then delivers structured data for the client’s own sales, partnerships, market research, or CRM workflow.

The service is research and data preparation. Islah does not run cold email, manage LinkedIn outreach, book appointments, or guarantee pipeline. **We Build the Data. Your Team Controls the Outreach.**

## Research shaped around your SaaS market

“SaaS” can cover horizontal platforms, vertical software, developer tools, infrastructure products, data services, cybersecurity products, and many other models. A useful brief therefore defines what belongs in the target market and what should be excluded. It may distinguish software vendors from consultancies, agencies, resellers, marketplaces, or companies that merely use software.

The project can begin from a new-market brief or from a named-account list. If new companies or people need to be discovered, the work is B2B Prospect Research. If your team already has records with missing, outdated, or inconsistent fields, Existing Database Enrichment is the appropriate service.

## Example ICP research criteria

ICP is used here only as shorthand for client-defined targeting criteria. Matching these observable criteria does not establish buying intent, budget, need, or readiness to purchase.

| Criteria area | Example definition to provide | Research treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Software category | Vertical SaaS, analytics, cybersecurity, HR technology, or another defined segment | Confirm from available company descriptions and sources |
| Business model | Subscription software, platform, licensed product, or specified model | Record only where reasonably supported |
| Geography | Headquarters, operating market, or contact location | Apply the exact geographic definition in the brief |
| Company size | Employee range or another agreed size measure | Record the source basis where included |
| Technology information | Named technologies or product integrations | Treat as time-sensitive and source-dependent |
| Funding information | Funding stage, disclosed round, or investor information | Record reported information without inferring budget |
| Hiring activity | Open roles in named functions | Treat as a client-defined signal, not proof of purchase intent |
| Target roles | Founder, executive, IT, operations, sales, finance, or another function | Research role relevance without claiming final authority |
| Exclusions | Agencies, services firms, subsidiaries, regions, or named accounts | Apply consistently and flag ambiguous cases |

A criteria table becomes more useful when each rule includes accepted alternatives. For example, a target role may allow a VP title first, a director title second, and a founder only for companies below a defined size. This prevents inconsistent judgment during research.

## Company and decision-maker research

Company research can identify SaaS businesses that match a specified category, geography, size range, and other observable characteristics. Decision-maker research can then locate people in relevant functions. The appropriate role depends on the client’s offer and target organization; it should not be assumed merely because a title sounds senior.

For named-account projects, the client can supply company names or domains and ask for specific functions. For market-building projects, Islah can research both the companies and the relevant people. Multiple contacts per account can be included when the brief calls for role coverage rather than a single record.

## Sample fields

A SaaS prospect-research dataset may include:

- Company name and website
- Company description or software category
- Headquarters or agreed location field
- Employee-size band where researchable
- Business model or customer focus where supported
- Named technology information where requested and available
- Publicly reported funding information where relevant
- Hiring signal and observation date when included
- Contact name
- Current title and function
- Seniority label based on the agreed taxonomy
- Public professional profile URL
- Business email and verification status where available
- Company or person source URLs
- Criteria-match notes
- Exclusion, uncertainty, or review status
- CRM owner, account ID, or client-supplied key

Not every field will be available for every company or person. The delivery should distinguish unavailable information from information that was not requested, and it should avoid filling gaps with guesses.

## From criteria to CRM-ready delivery

The process starts by converting the brief into research rules. Category definitions, company-size measures, locations, role priorities, exclusions, and required fields should be agreed. A sample can then test whether the rules produce the expected kinds of records.

During research, sources may disagree or describe companies at different levels of specificity. Ambiguous records can be flagged for client review instead of forced into a category. Where technology, hiring, funding, or role data is time-sensitive, a source or observation date can help the client interpret it.

Delivery can follow an agreed spreadsheet or CSV structure. Values should be standardized, duplicate records reviewed, and statuses kept consistent. If a client supplies CRM IDs, those identifiers can remain attached to the corresponding records so the completed data can be mapped back safely.

## New discovery versus database enrichment

Choose B2B Prospect Research when you need to discover a new set of SaaS companies, expand a named-account list with relevant people, or build a database for a new segment or territory.

Choose Existing Database Enrichment when you already have SaaS records and need missing domains, categories, titles, profiles, business emails, or company details researched; when values need standardization; or when likely duplicates need review.

A combined project can contain both workstreams, but newly discovered and client-supplied records should have clear source labels. That makes review, import, and later maintenance easier.

## Data quality and limitations

Public company descriptions can be broad, stale, or promotional. Technology signals can change. Funding details may be reported at different times or in different currencies. Employee estimates can vary by source. Titles can change between research and outreach. These limitations should be handled with transparent statuses and dates rather than hidden behind a single “qualified” label.

A criteria-match label means the record matched the defined observable rules based on the research available. It is not an intent score. A hiring signal, funding announcement, or technology observation does not prove that a company needs the client’s offer or is ready to change providers.

Business email verification can be included where available. Verification reduces some uncertainty at the time of checking, but it does not guarantee future validity, deliverability, a reply, or a commercial outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you research SaaS companies in a specific niche?

Yes. Define the software category, included and excluded business models, geography, size range, and any other observable criteria. Ambiguous companies can be flagged for review.

### Can you find people within our named accounts?

Yes. Provide the account list and define required functions, seniority, acceptable alternatives, and the number of contacts needed per account. Role relevance does not establish final purchasing authority.

### Do you provide technology-stack or funding data?

These fields can be researched when included in scope and available from appropriate sources. They are time-sensitive and should not be treated as proof of budget or intent.

### Are emails verified?

Business emails can be researched and verified where available. The delivery can include a verification status, but no method guarantees inbox placement or response.

### Can you clean our existing SaaS database?

Yes. Existing Database Enrichment can complete missing fields, review titles, standardize values, flag duplicates, and verify business emails where available for records you already own.

### Will you contact the prospects for us?

No. Islah provides the research dataset. Your team controls messaging, outreach channels, sequencing, qualification, and follow-up.
